Research

Improving programming proficiency through NumCode.

A proposed quasi-experimental study at Unida Christian Colleges, Imus City, Cavite.

Why this study

Research on introductory programming reports difficulties with syntax, concepts and problem-solving. Structured digital support may help, but each tool needs to be evaluated in its own context. This study evaluates NumCode with selected Grade 12 ICT Programming students.

Coding proficiency here means students' assessed performance on the selected HTML, CSS and Java competencies covered by NumCode, not mastery of every language.

Research questions

  1. What is the students' coding proficiency level before using NumCode (pre-test)?
  2. What is it after using NumCode (post-test)?
  3. Is the difference between pre-test and post-test scores statistically significant?

Method

Study design

Design

Quasi-experimental, comparing the same students' scores before and after using NumCode.

Respondents

Grade 12 ICT Programming students at UCC, chosen by purposive sampling with set criteria.

Procedure

Consent, pre-test, one week of NumCode use, then a post-test with similar questions.

Instrument

Coding Proficiency Assessment

A researcher-made test worth 21 points in total, validated by the research adviser and qualified validators.

PartWhat it coversPoints
I. ProfileRespondent code, date, grade level, pre-test or post-test—
II. Knowledge15 multiple-choice questions (5 each: HTML, CSS, Java)15
III. Application3 coding tasks (one each: HTML, CSS, Java), 2 points each6
Total21

Data analysis

Mean and standard deviation summarize pre-test and post-test scores. A suitable test for paired scores may be used to check whether the difference is statistically significant.

Ethics & privacy

  • Informed consent and assent; voluntary participation
  • No effect on grades; scores are not counted academically
  • Participants identified by codes; group reporting only
  • Follows the Data Privacy Act of 2012 (RA 10173)
